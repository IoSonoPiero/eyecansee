# EyeCanSee manual

Press **F1** at any time to open or close this manual.

## The window

- **Menu bar**: File, Edit, Image, View, Tools, Help. The version number is on the right; **Help → About EyeCanSee** shows the version, the author, the license and the link to the source code. Every action you can do with the keyboard or a double click also has a menu item, with its key shown next to it; the items have the same icon as their button, also in the right-click menu.
- **Toolbar**: parent folder (in the viewer: back to the grid) and refresh; the operations in groups: **File** (new folder, cut, copy, paste, rename, delete), **Image** (turn left and right, edit image, save, save as) and **Tools** (convert and resize, find duplicates); then metadata, the address bar, the image order and the thumbnail size slider. Pointing at a button shows its name and key; on one that is off, also why it cannot be used. When the window is narrow and the address bar would be left too short, the Image and Tools groups are in the **⋯** menu. The buttons have [Lucide](https://lucide.dev) icons; folders, devices and files have the coloured ones.
- **Folder tree** on the left, under **This computer** (and under **Devices** if you have any connected): click a folder to show its content. Folders open only when you expand them, so remote sources stay fast. When you change folder, also from the grid, the tree opens down to that folder and highlights it.
- **Grid** on the right: the **..** cell to go up one level (not at the top of a disk or device), the subfolders, then the images as thumbnails, spread over the whole width. When you enter a folder nothing is selected; when you go up, the folder you came from is selected.
- **Image order** from the drop-down list in the toolbar (with the ▲/▼ button next to it to reverse it) or from **View → Sort by**: by name, modification date, size or type (extension), plus **Descending order**. Folders stay first, by name; files without a date stay at the end. The choice is remembered.
- **Metadata** below the grid, for the selected image: boxes **File** (name, size on disk also in bytes, file date and pixels, for every file, also without metadata), **Camera** (make, model, lens, software), **Shot** (date, exposure, aperture, ISO, focal length, flash), **Image** (format, size, colour and bits per channel, colour space; format, size and colour also for images without EXIF) and **Location** (GPS coordinates, altitude, with a link to open them on the OpenStreetMap map). Each box closes with its X and reopens from **View → Metadata boxes**; the ℹ button in the toolbar closes or reopens them all. The panel collapses and reopens with the ▼/▲ button at the right of its bar, or with a click on the bar itself; when collapsed only the bar stays, and the metadata is not read. EXIF metadata is read from JPEG, HEIC, AVIF, PNG, WebP and TIFF; to read it EyeCanSee reads the file again, so on a phone it takes a moment; once read, it is kept with the thumbnails and shows at once the next time (until the file changes; emptying the thumbnail cache empties it too).
- **Status bar** at the bottom: how many folders and images there are, and name, size and modification date of the selected item; with several items selected, how many and the size of their images.
- **Several items**: Ctrl+click adds or removes an item, Shift+click selects everything from the last clicked item to this one, Shift+arrows extend the selection, Ctrl+A selects everything in the folder. **Dragging** with the mouse draws a rectangle that selects the cells it touches (with Ctrl it adds them to those already selected); near the top or bottom edge the grid scrolls. A click without keys, or an arrow, goes back to a single item.
- Arrows and Tab act on the side you clicked last, left or right: it is the one with the thin frame. There the selection is solid blue, on the other side it is lighter.

Double-click an image, or select it and press Enter, to open it in the **viewer**. Press Esc, double-click the image or use the grid button in the toolbar to go back to the grid. In the viewer the toolbar keeps only what works on the image shown (rename, delete, turn, edit, save); new folder, cut, copy, paste and the tools are for the grid, and stay in the menus. The bar at the bottom of the viewer shows the file name, its position in the folder (for example 12 / 51), its size in pixels and on disk, and the date it was taken (the date it was modified when the photo has no such information or the metadata boxes are closed).

## Thumbnails

EyeCanSee creates the thumbnails in this order: the selected image first, then the ones visible in the window from top to bottom, then all the others in the folder from top to bottom. Clicking an image moves its thumbnail to the front of the queue. Up to 1000 thumbnails per folder stay in memory, so they do not disappear while scrolling, even when dragging the scrollbar; in bigger folders the ones far from the visible part are reloaded from disk.

While a thumbnail is being made its tile shows a turning circle, with the video sign under it for a video. On a phone, a server or a disk other than the computer's own, the videos come after the pictures: there a video is often read whole for its picture. Thumbnails are saved on disk, so opening the same folder again is immediate: in the file `~/.cache/eyecansee/thumbs.db` on Linux and `%LOCALAPPDATA%\eyecansee\thumbs.db` on Windows. If an image changes (size or modification date), its thumbnail is created again. You can delete that file (with EyeCanSee closed) at any time: thumbnails are created again when needed.

**Clean up the thumbnail cache** in **Tools > Options…** (off by default): once per session, for each opened location, removes thumbnails of deleted images and recreates those of changed images; then removes the least recently used thumbnails until the cache is within **Cache limit** (default 1024 MB). Folders that cannot be found or reached are skipped, so a disconnected disk or a switched-off NAS does not lose its thumbnails. Remote locations are checked only when you open them.

## Options

**Tools > Options…** opens the options window, divided into tabs: **General** (last folder, window at startup, language, updates), **Look** (colors), **Browsing** (scrolling, viewer), **Thumbnails**, **Deleting**, **Slideshow** and **Video** (video engine and FFmpeg). Every change is saved immediately in `~/.config/eyecansee/settings.json` on Linux and `%APPDATA%\eyecansee\settings.json` on Windows, a text file you can also edit by hand.

- **Reopen the last folder at startup** (on by default): when EyeCanSee starts without a folder, for example from the applications menu, it opens the folder you were viewing when you closed it. If that folder no longer exists, it opens your home folder. Remote locations reopen with the password only when the server is among the saved servers (**File > Server manager…**): otherwise, for an FTP server with a user, **File > Open location…** opens with everything filled in except the password, ready to be typed (Cancel stays in your home folder).
- **Remember the scroll position of each folder** (off by default): when you go back to a folder you already viewed, the grid starts where you left it. When off, every folder you open starts from the first thumbnail. It lasts until EyeCanSee is closed.
- **In the viewer, after the last image start again from the first** (off by default): and from the first image, going back shows the last one. When off, the viewer stops at the first and the last image.
- **In the viewer, the window's ✕ goes back to the grid** (on by default): the ✕ at the top right does as Esc. When off, the ✕ closes EyeCanSee from the viewer too.
- **Mouse wheel in the viewer**: **Zooms in and out** (default) or **Shows the next or previous image**. Holding Ctrl, the wheel always zooms.
- **Largest zoom in the viewer** (2000% at first): how far the wheel and + can enlarge an image.
- **Window at startup**, one choice only: **Normal size**, **Maximized** (fills the screen, leaving the system bars visible), or **Size it had when closed** (if it was maximized, it reopens maximized).
- **Check for updates at startup** (on by default): see *Updates*.
- **Language**: **Same as the system** (default: the Windows or desktop language, English if it is not among the available ones) or one of the listed languages, each shown by its own name. The choice applies at once, this manual included.
- **Colors**: **Same as the system** (default: light or dark like the Windows or desktop theme, following it when it changes), **Light** or **Dark**. The choice applies at once.
- **Clean up the thumbnail cache** and **Cache limit**: see *Thumbnails*.
- **Empty the thumbnail cache…**: deletes all saved thumbnails, after a confirmation showing how many and how much space is freed. Your images are not touched; thumbnails of the open folder are created again right away, the others when you open them again.
- **Delete files on this computer for good, without the trash (dangerous)** (off at first): see *Deleting and renaming*.
- **Deleting on phones and servers**: see *Deleting and renaming*.
- **Slideshow**: how long each image stays, the passage between images and whether it starts again after the last one: see *Slideshow*.
- **Video engine (libmpv) and FFmpeg to use** (Video tab): **Automatic: the best for this computer** (default: the system's when it is recent enough, otherwise the downloaded one; for FFmpeg also the downloaded one when it does more of the work on the graphics card, as on Fedora with an NVIDIA card), **Always the system's, whatever its version**, or **Always the downloaded one**. EyeCanSee needs at least libmpv 1.109 (mpv 0.33) and FFmpeg 4.3; on Linux the distribution's packages come first because they are better integrated with the graphics card. The tab also shows which ones are in use now, with their versions. A change applies the next time EyeCanSee starts. See *Videos* and *Converting and resizing images and videos*.

## Updates

Installed from the Microsoft Store, EyeCanSee is updated by the Store and does not look for updates by itself. The Windows installer, the Windows zip and the AppImage update themselves. At startup EyeCanSee asks GitHub, in the background, for the latest published version; if it is newer, it downloads it and checks it twice: against the SHA-256 fingerprint GitHub publishes for it (a broken download), and against the developer's signature, made with a key that never leaves the developer's PC (a file that does not come from the developer). A file without a valid signature is never installed, and Check for updates says why. When it is ready, a red **Install … and restart** button appears at the top right of the menu bar: EyeCanSee closes, installs the new version and opens again. Nothing is installed until you click it.

- **Help > Check for updates** does the same check at any time and tells you the outcome.
- The check can be turned off in **Tools > Options… > General**. When off, EyeCanSee contacts GitHub only when you choose Check for updates. Nothing about you or your images is sent: only the request for the latest version.
- On Fedora, EyeCanSee is updated by `dnf` with the rest of the system.
- As a Flatpak, EyeCanSee is updated through `flatpak update` or your software center (for example GNOME Software, Discover or the Linux Mint Update Manager), and does not check for updates by itself.

## Deleting and renaming

**File > New folder…**, **New folder…** from the right click on an empty spot of the grid, or **Ctrl+Shift+N** makes a folder in the folder open: the name comes already written ("New folder", with " (2)" when taken) and selected, so you can type the one you want; **Create** makes it and selects it in the grid. From the right click on a folder (in the grid or the tree), **New folder inside…** makes it inside that folder. It works on the computer, on disks, phones and servers; not in a trash nor among the storages of a phone.

**Del** (or **File > Delete…**) deletes the image or folder selected in the grid, or the image open in the viewer, after a confirmation showing its name and size. A folder is deleted with everything inside it. Folders in the tree on the left are deleted with a right click. In the confirmation **Enter** deletes and **Esc** cancels. When a folder is among the items, EyeCanSee reads its first level (not the subfolders) as the confirmation opens, and the confirmation button (**Move to trash**, or **Delete** when it is for good) waits a moment for it: if the folder contains files or folders the grid does not show (documents, hidden files and the like), which would be deleted too, the confirmation says so with an example; if it cannot be read, it says that too. With a folder **Enter** cancels, like **Esc**. In the viewer the next image appears at once.

With several items selected, **Del** (or **Delete…** from the right click on one of them) deletes all of them, after a confirmation with how many they are and the size of the images. If one fails, the following ones are not deleted. While a deletion runs the status bar says so, with a spinner, and no other deletion can be started (on a phone a folder with hundreds of files takes several seconds).

- **Files on this computer and external disks** go to the system trash, where they can be restored. On Windows, USB sticks, SD cards and network drives have no Recycle Bin: there the image goes into the hidden `.EyeCanSee-trash` folder next to it, as on phones (see below), and the confirmation says so. With the option **Delete files on this computer for good, without the trash** they are deleted forever.
- **Phones and servers (FTP, SFTP)** have no trash EyeCanSee can reach. In the Options, under **Deleting on phones and servers**, choose between **Move to the hidden .EyeCanSee-trash folder next to the image** (default: the image goes into a `.EyeCanSee-trash` folder inside its own folder, and you get it back with a file manager) and **Delete for good**.

**Shift+Del** (or **File > Delete for good…**) deletes forever that one time, whatever the option says.

**F2** (or **File > Rename…**) renames the selected item or the open image: type the new name and press **Enter** (**Esc** cancels). EyeCanSee does not accept a name already in the folder. Rename works on one item at a time: with several selected it is off.

The top folder of a source and the storages of a phone cannot be deleted or renamed.

**The trash of phones and servers** (and of USB sticks on Windows): every folder where you deleted something has a **Trash**: a strip at the top of the grid says so, with the **Open the trash** button. Inside it the strip says whose trash it is and has **Restore all** and **Empty the trash…**. Inside it you can look at the images, **restore** them (they go back to the folder containing the trash) or delete them for good: there **Del** always deletes forever, and there is no Rename. Right click on an empty space inside the Trash: **Restore all** and **Empty the trash…** (everything deleted forever, after a confirmation). A trash left empty, after restoring or deleting what was inside it, goes away together with its strip. An empty `.EyeCanSee-trash` folder (copied from another device, for example) shows no strip. A folder moved to another device, or merged into a folder with the same name, takes its trash along (an item whose name is already in the trash there becomes "name (2)"); a copy leaves the trash out. On Android phones the trash of a folder also shows the photos and videos the phone's gallery put in its own trash (hidden `.trashed-…` files, which Android deletes by itself after 30 days: the scheme of Google Photos and Files by Google; the Xiaomi Gallery keeps its own bin elsewhere, which EyeCanSee does not see): they appear with their real name, are restored like the others (back to their name in the same folder) and deleting them there deletes them for good; **Empty the trash…** deletes them too and removes the `.EyeCanSee-trash` folder, **Restore all** renames them all and removes the folder if it is left empty.

If the name of an item being restored is already taken, EyeCanSee asks what to do: for a file **Replace** (the one already there is deleted) or **Keep both** (the restored one becomes for example `photo (2).jpg`); for a folder **Merge** (the contents add up; files with the same name are both kept) or **Keep both**. **Cancel** also stops the rest of a Restore all. In the trash too a name already there becomes `photo (2).jpg`.

## Finding duplicates

**Tools > Find duplicates…**, or **Find duplicates…** from the right click on a folder (or on an empty space of the grid, for the open folder), looks for identical files: photos, videos and audio files, the same file byte for byte, even under another name or in another folder. Files that were resized, converted or saved again are not identical and are not found. It works on every source: computer, external disks, phones and servers.

First choose whether to look **Also in subfolders** (on by default), then **Search** (**Enter**; **Esc** cancels). A window shows how many files have been examined out of those found and how much has been read; **Cancel** stops the search. Only files with the same size are read and compared, so on a computer it is quick; on a phone every image that could be a copy is read in full, and it takes time.

The results fill the window. Every group of identical files has its thumbnail (click it to open the image in the viewer; **Esc** brings you back to the results) and one line per copy, with its date and its path below the folder searched (the full path when you point at it). The box on each line means **delete this copy** (a click on the text of the line ticks or unticks it too): the copies to delete are faded and struck through, the one that stays says **Stays**. At least one copy of every group always stays: the last box left unticked cannot be ticked.

At the start every group keeps its first copy. **Order** decides which one is first, for all the groups: **Date, oldest first** (to the minute, as shown; with the same date, the shorter path), **Shortest name** (copies usually have something added to the name, such as "photo copy.jpg" or "photo (2).jpg": so the original stays even when the dates are the same; when the names are as long, as Date) or **Path** (alphabetical order of folder and name). **Keep the first**, **Keep the last** and **Keep all** mark every group at once; then you can change single boxes. The line at the bottom says how many groups there are, how many copies will be deleted and how much space is freed. **Delete the marked copies…** asks for confirmation and deletes them as **Del** does (trash, the folder's Trash or for good, as in the Options). **Close** or **Esc** closes the results.

With the keyboard: **↑** and **↓** move between the copies of all the groups, **Space** ticks or unticks, **Esc** closes. Deleting is done with the **Delete the marked copies…** button: Enter does not delete.

## Saving in another format

Right-click an image (in the grid or in the viewer) and choose **Save as…** (with one file selected; for several there is *Convert and resize*). A page takes the whole window: the original on the left, the converted image on the right, each with its size in pixels and on disk (for the converted one also as a percentage of the original). What you see on the right is the file as it will be saved: it is encoded and decoded back every time you change an option. While that happens the size says "Working out the size…" and Save waits: the size shown is always that of the file that will be saved.

- **Format**: JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, TIFF, BMP, GIF, QOI, ICO, TGA, PNM, OpenEXR, Radiance HDR or Farbfeld, with all the options of each one: quality, colour detail, progressive and lossless JPEG (for a JPEG original, its own quality and colour detail are proposed, read from the file; otherwise 90 and 4:2:0); compression level and filter for PNG; lossy or lossless WebP; quality, speed and bit depth for AVIF; compression for TIFF; colour reduction for GIF; compression for TGA. An icon (ICO) holds at most 256 × 256 pixels, so a larger image is reduced; JPEG and HDR have no transparency, so transparent parts become white: the page says so. These six formats are only for Save as: Save writes back over the original only the first eight. The full table is in [docs/formats.md](https://github.com/IoSonoPiero/eyecansee/blob/main/docs/formats.md).
- **100%** shows the real pixels; drag either image to move both, so you compare the same spot.
- **Keep the metadata (EXIF, XMP, IPTC, colour profile)** is on at first. It is greyed out when the original has none or the format cannot hold them (JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and TIFF can). The EXIF is kept, also from HEIC photos and from RAW photos of every maker (camera, lens, shot, GPS, author and copyright; not the maker's private notes, the maker note); a RAW photo also gets the colour profile of the image EyeCanSee develops from it (sRGB). Files of the rare formats read through ImageMagick keep their EXIF, XMP, IPTC and colour profile; GIMP files (XCF) only their colour profile. XMP and IPTC (author, copyright, licence, descriptions, keywords) are kept; WebP and AVIF have no place for IPTC, so its fields go into the XMP. The orientation is set to upright, since the saved pixels are already turned, and the sizes written in the EXIF and the XMP are those of the new image. Left out: the small previews that cameras and Photoshop store in the file (they showed the original, the part cropped away included, and programs make their own from the image), and the Lightroom and Camera Raw settings, which describe a crop and adjustments already in the pixels and could be applied a second time; and, for a phone photo, the description of the HDR map and of the motion photo video, which the new file does not have (Convert and resize does the same).
- **File name** and **Save**: the new file goes into the same folder as the original. If the name is already taken, " (2)" is added, and the page says so before you save.
- Esc or the window's ✕ closes the page without saving.

## Converting and resizing images and videos

Select images and videos in the grid (or right-click one of the selected ones) and choose **Tools > Convert and resize…** or **Convert and resize…** from the right-click menu. It works on pictures and videos: the other files chosen (3D models, CAD drawings, audio) are left out, and the window says how many. A window asks:

**Tools > Copy with the metadata…** (in the right click too, when a video is among the files chosen) opens the same window already set on **Same as the original**: the video is rewritten without re-encoding, in seconds, with its metadata inside (for dashcams and drones the position taken from the files beside the video too); you only choose where to put it.

**Tools > Copy without sound…** and **Tools > Save the sound as a file…** (in the right click too, when a video is among the files chosen) open the same window on the videos chosen, with only the destination to choose (at first next to the originals, which are never touched):

- **Copy without sound** makes a new video, the same as the original but without sound; the original is not touched. It writes the video without its audio track: the picture and the other tracks (subtitles, chapters) are copied as they are, in the format of the original, without re-encoding and with the metadata. The new file gets a free name, for example `video (2).mp4`.
- **Save the sound as a file** saves the sound of the video in a separate file; the video is not touched. It writes every audio track into a file of its own, copied as it is, in the format of its codec: AAC as `.m4a`, Opus as `.opus`, MP3 as `.mp3`, FLAC as `.flac`, Vorbis as `.ogg`, others as `.mka`. A video with two tracks gives `video.m4a` and `video-2.m4a`. For another format (for example MP3 from AAC), use **Convert and resize**.

Both work on the computer, on phones and on servers (the video is copied to the computer, worked on, and the new files written back), and keep the metadata, a dashcam's or a drone's position included. A video without audio is left out and named at the end.

- **Format**: one of those of *Save in another format*, with its options under **Options**, or **Same as the original**: each image in its own format, written as Save writes it (a JPEG at its own quality, see below); images in a format EyeCanSee cannot write (HEIC, RAW…) are then left out. An **Ultra HDR** photo written as JPEG (Same as the original, or JPEG) stays Ultra HDR, also resized or turned: its HDR map is resized and turned with it; the video of a motion photo is kept only when it is just turned. In the other formats, or without *Keep the metadata*, the HDR is lost: when the photos chosen include JPEGs, the window says so under the format.
- **Videos** (only when videos are among the files chosen): **container** (MP4, MKV, WebM, MOV, AVI, GIF), **codec** (H.264, HEVC, AV1, VP9, VP8, MPEG-4, ProRes, GIF, or **Same as the original**: video and sound copied without re-encoding, in seconds and without loss, with the metadata written into the new file; the turn and the size do not apply) and **encoder** (those of the graphics card in green, with a line that says so), with a line on what the one chosen is good for; **quality** (High, Medium, Small file); **Keep the sound**; **Keep the metadata** (date, place, camera; for dashcams with `gps.sqlite` and drones with `.SRT` the new file gets the starting point, and for dashcams the altitude and average and top speed too, so the panel shows them away from the card as well). Videos are converted by FFmpeg: the system's one when there is one and it is at least version 4.3 (an older one is reported with its version, and the download offered), otherwise the window offers to download it (63 MB on Windows, 67 MB on Linux, once, into the app's data folder; its SHA-256 is checked). The first time EyeCanSee tries every encoder and shows only those that work on that computer: first the graphics card ones (NVIDIA, Intel Quick Sync, AMD, Windows, VA-API on Linux), much faster, then the processor ones, slower but with slightly smaller files at the same quality. With an NVIDIA, Intel Quick Sync or VA-API encoder the card also reads, turns and resizes the video where it can, and the processor stays almost free; a video the card cannot read is converted again on the processor by itself. Where the system's FFmpeg leaves part of that work to the processor and the option is Automatic, the window says so and offers to download EyeCanSee's, used from the next start. Codecs without a working encoder stay grey. The full table is in [docs/video-formats.md](https://github.com/IoSonoPiero/eyecansee/blob/main/docs/video-formats.md). Videos on a phone or a server are copied into a temporary file first.
- **Turn and flip**: a quarter turn, half a turn, and horizontal or vertical flips; the flips come first, then the turn. Only turned and in their own format, JPEGs are turned without losing quality when their size allows it, as with Save.
- **Size**: unchanged, a percentage of the original, or inside a box of pixels keeping the proportions (after the turn). Smaller files are not enlarged unless you ask. For images, the **filter** decides how the pixels are worked out: Lanczos is the sharpest, Nearest pixel keeps the hard edges of pixel art.
- **Where**: next to the originals, in a subfolder (created if missing), or in another folder, also on another device: **Browse…** opens an EyeCanSee window to choose the folder, with the home folder, the drives, the connected devices (phones too) and the saved servers on the left: a click on a folder goes into it, **Use this folder** chooses it, or you write an address as in the address bar (`mtp://…` for a phone, `sftp://…`); that folder must exist. If the folder written no longer opens (say the phone was unplugged), the window starts at the home folder. So photos of the phone can be converted straight into a folder of the computer. The originals are never touched; a name already taken gets " (2)".

**Convert** starts the work; a box in the corner shows how far it got, and the program can be used meanwhile. The bar moves during a long video too. **Cancel** stops at once (the half-made video is deleted). At the end a message says how many files were saved and where, and which were left out and why: animated images and images with several pages (they would keep only the first one). Phone photos with an HDR map or a motion photo video are converted: the new file is the picture, the original keeps the rest. It works on every device: computer, disks, phone.

## Slideshow

**View > Slideshow**, or **Slideshow** in the right-click menu of an image (in the grid and in the viewer), shows the images one after the other at full screen, on black. With several images selected it shows those, always from the first in the grid; otherwise every image of the folder, from the chosen one (or the one open in the viewer).

- **Esc** or a double click ends it: you are back where you were, on the last image shown. **Space** pauses and goes on. **←** and **→** (or Page Up and Page Down) show the previous and next image at once; with **Slide**, going back the images slide the other way.
- In **Tools > Options… > Slideshow**: how many seconds each image stays (5 at first), the passage between images (**None**, **Crossfade**, the default, **Fade through black**, **Slide**, **Zoom and fade**), and **Start again after the last image** (on at first; off, the slideshow ends after the last one).
- The next image is read in advance: if it is not ready yet (a phone, a large file) the current one stays until it is.

## Videos

Videos (MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM, AVI and others) appear in the grid next to the images, with a frame of the video as thumbnail and a ▶ badge. When the camera stored a preview inside the video (dashcams such as Umino do, and DJI drones as a cover) the thumbnail is that preview, ready almost at once. They are played by mpv. On Linux EyeCanSee uses the system's libmpv when there is one (`mpv-libs` on Fedora, which the Fedora package asks for by itself, `libmpv2` on Debian, Ubuntu and openSUSE, `libmpv1` on Ubuntu 22.04, `mpv` on Arch) and it is recent enough: libmpv 1.109, that is mpv 0.33 or later (an older one is reported with its version, and the download is offered). Otherwise, and always on Windows, the viewer offers to download the video engine the first time a video is opened (10 MB on Linux, 47 MB on Windows, once; it goes in the app's data folder and its SHA-256 is checked before use), and the video plays at once. Which one to use (the system's, the downloaded one, or the system's only when recent enough) is chosen under *Options*, Video tab. There is no need to open a video: as soon as the open folder holds a video and the engine is missing, the red **Download the video engine** button appears at the top right (as the update one does), downloads it and then turns into **Restart EyeCanSee**. Until then a video's thumbnail is the camera's preview when it has one, otherwise the video sign with a download arrow: nothing is copied from a phone to make one.

Audio files (M4A, MP3, Opus, Ogg, FLAC, WAV, MKA and others, for example those made by **Save the sound as a file**) appear in the grid too: with their cover, if they have one, and a ♪ badge, otherwise with a note. The viewer plays them with the same engine, with the same bar and volume; a cover shows as the picture.

- **Enter** or a double click opens the video in the viewer, where it starts playing with sound, fitted to the window.
- **Space**, a click on the video or the button under it play and pause. The bar under the video shows the time and moves along it: drag it or click a point. At the end the last frame stays; play starts again from the beginning.
- The volume is at the right of the bar: the slider (0 to 100) and the button that turns the sound off and on. **↑** and **↓** raise and lower it by 5, and so does the mouse wheel over the video; **M** turns the sound off and on. Volume and sound off stay for the next videos and the next time EyeCanSee starts. On a video without sound the two controls are greyed out.
- **←** and **→** go to the previous and next item, as for images; a double click or **Esc** goes back to the grid.
- The metadata panel shows a selected video's details when the file has them: make, model and Android version of the phone, date and time of recording, length, frame rate, size, video and audio format, bit rate, and the location (phones and iPhones write it). Dashcams that keep their GPS track in a `gps.sqlite` file on the card (such as Umino) show where the video starts, the altitude, and the average and top speed during the video; a copy of the video made by hand without that file has no location (Convert and resize writes it into the new file). DJI drones write the position in a `.SRT` file with the same name as the video: the panel shows where the video starts, taken from its first frame; if the drone had no GPS fix yet when recording started, the video has no location rather than a wrong one.
- **View > Show**, or **Show** in the right-click menu of the empty part of the grid: **Images, videos and audio** (the default), **Images only**, **Videos only** or **Audio only**. The choice stays for every folder until you change it; the status bar counts images, videos and audio files apart.
- Videos on a phone or a server are read a piece at a time while they play, never copied whole. The exception is a phone on Windows, which cannot jump into the middle of a file: the video is first copied whole to a temporary file and plays after that, with a bar showing how much has been read. Copying, moving, renaming and deleting work as for images. Save and the turns in the viewer are for images only: videos are turned and converted from *Convert and resize*. In the slideshow a video plays whole, with sound, then the next item comes; **Space** pauses it too.

## 3D models

3D models made of triangles appear in the grid next to the images, with a picture of the model as thumbnail: **STL** and **3MF** (3D printing), **OBJ** (with the colours of its materials, the `.mtl` file next to it), **PLY** (also 3D scans; point clouds without faces show as dots), **glTF** (`.glb`, and `.gltf` with its `.bin` file), **FBX**, **Collada** (`.dae`, and `.zae` zipped), **3DS** (3D Studio), **OFF**, **AMF** (3D printing), **VRML** and **X3D** (`.wrl`, `.x3d`, `.x3dv`, also gzipped, with the files they include from next to them; lines and points as thin strokes and dots) **IFC** (building models, BIM: walls, slabs, windows, structures, pipes, roads and bridges in their colours) and the solid models of mechanical CAD programs, **STEP**, **IGES** and **BREP**, with their colours and assemblies: the first one opened offers to download the CAD engine (Open CASCADE, 8 MB on Windows, 11 MB on Linux, once), and the button in the menu bar does too; until then their thumbnail is a cube with a download arrow, and nothing is read for it from a phone or a server. Enter or a double click opens the model in the viewer, drawn by the graphics card, where it can be turned and looked at from every side:

- **Arrows**: turn the model about the axes of the screen, all the way round (hold them down to keep turning; two arrows together turn it along the diagonal). Dragging with the mouse turns it too.
- **Shift** and drag, or drag with the middle button: move the model in the window.
- Mouse wheel, **+** and **−**: zoom in and out; **0** goes back to the starting view.
- **Page Down**, **Space** and **Page Up**, **Backspace**: next and previous item (the arrows turn the model instead); **Esc** or a double click: back to the grid.
- **Ctrl+S** (or *Save in another format*): the model as it is shown, as an image, at least 1920 pixels wide, with the same formats and options as for photos.

Right click (or **File**) > **Export as a 3D model** saves the models chosen (one or more) as STL, OBJ, PLY or glTF (GLB), each next to itself, with as much of the original as the format holds: glTF (GLB) keeps the textures, normals and surfaces, the other formats get the colours (taken from the textures where there are any); each file has the up axis other programs expect (Z for STL and PLY, Y for OBJ and glTF), so it opens standing there too, and the axes in the viewer's corner may differ from the original's; the status bar says the name it got.

In the viewer the **Keys** button on the right of the status bar lists the keys of what you are looking at (an image, a video, a 3D model or a CAD drawing), one a row: while the mouse is on it, or until it is clicked again. In the top right corner the axes of the file (X red, Y green, Z blue) turn with the model, as in CAD programs; a CAD drawing shows its X and Y in the bottom left corner.

The colours stored in the file are shown (colours of materials, faces and vertices) and, in glTF, 3MF, OBJ, FBX, Collada, 3DS, VRML and X3D files, the pictures on the surfaces (textures); glTF and FBX materials also show how smooth or metallic they are (with a highlight), their bumps (normal maps), shadows in their creases (occlusion) and their own light. Curved surfaces look smooth and edges stay sharp, also in models that do not store their normals (scans, STL); a model without colours is light grey. The status bar shows the number of triangles, the zoom and, while the model moves, the frames per second. *Convert and resize* turns several models into images at once, seen from the starting view.

## CAD drawings

AutoCAD drawings, **DWG** (from R13 to 2018 and later) and **DXF** (text and binary), and plotter files, **HP-GL** and **HP-GL/2** (`.plt`, `.hpgl`, also inside a PCL print job, with the colours, widths and line types of the pens and their user units), appear in the grid with a picture of the drawing as thumbnail and open in the viewer like an image: zoom with the mouse wheel, + and −, move by dragging, *Save in another format* to make an image of them. The drawing is seen from above, as in the model space of a CAD program, on its dark background: lines, arcs, circles, polylines, splines, ellipses, texts, dimensions, blocks (also inside other blocks), tables and hatches (solid, or with their pattern lines), in their colours, line types (dashes) and line weights; hidden and frozen layers are left out; pictures attached to the drawing are shown when their file is next to it. Texts are written with the drawing's SHX fonts (AutoCAD's single-stroke letters, also the big fonts for Chinese, Japanese and Korean) when the font files are next to the drawing, in a `fonts` folder there or, on Windows, in an AutoCAD installation; otherwise with a system font. Once the view stops, the part on screen is drawn again from the drawing, so thin lines and small texts stay sharp at any zoom. The status bar shows the drawing's size in its unit (as AutoCAD stored it; millimetres for a plot; from 10000 mm up in metres), not pixels, which a drawing does not have. A drawing with layouts (paper space) shows **Model** and the name of the layout in the status bar: **L** goes to the next layout and **Shift+L** to the one before, each with its sheet, title block and viewports onto the model at their scale (the layers frozen in a viewport are left out of it).

**Tools > Export drawing…** (in the right click too) saves the drawings chosen in the grid as pictures or as SVG, all at once: they do not go through *Convert and resize*, since a drawing has no pixels of its own to resize. Choose the **format** (one of those of *Save in another format*, with its options, or **SVG**, sharp at any zoom, without layers or blocks), the **long side** in pixels (from 256 to 16384: the drawing is drawn again at that size, so it stays sharp even when large) and **where** the files go, as in *Convert and resize*. Pictures and SVG show the model space, not the layouts. **DWG** and **DXF** save the whole drawing in the other AutoCAD format (a DWG as DXF and the other way round), in the version it was saved with, with its layers, blocks, layouts and styles: each new file is read back and compared with the original, and if anything would be lost (a layout, some elements) it is not saved and the summary says so.

## Rotating and flipping

In the viewer, the **Image** menu flips the image horizontally or vertically and rotates it by 90° clockwise (Ctrl+R) or anticlockwise (Ctrl+Shift+R) or by 180°; the two rotations are also buttons on the toolbar, and everything is in the right-click menu. They are for images: on a 3D model or a CAD drawing they are off (a model turns with the mouse and the arrows). The change is only shown until you save it (the status bar says "Changed, not saved", in yellow). Leaving the image with the change not saved (another image, back to the grid, the parent folder, closing EyeCanSee) first asks whether to **Save**, **Save as…** or **Don't save**; **Cancel** stays on the image. Edit image asks the same way.

- **File > Save** (Ctrl+S) saves over the original, after asking: first it prepares the new file (the status bar says "Preparing to save…"), so the question can say whether anything is lost; the original goes where a deletion would send it (the trash, usually). A JPEG is rotated without losing quality, as `jpegtran` does, when its width and height are multiples of 16 pixels (8 for some); otherwise it is encoded again at the quality and colour subsampling it was saved with, read from the file (as ImageMagick estimates it; 92 when it cannot be read), so it is not compressed harder. The editor's Save copy and Overwrite do the same for a JPEG; a picture of another format saved as JPEG is written at quality 95. Either way the metadata (EXIF, XMP, IPTC, colour profile) stay, with the new sizes and without the small previews of the original and the Lightroom settings, as in Save as. PNG, WebP, AVIF, TIFF, BMP, GIF and QOI are encoded again in their own format. The confirmation says when the image will be encoded again with some loss of quality (a JPEG of other sizes, lossy WebP, AVIF, GIF). A PSD is saved as a PSD, with its colour profile, EXIF, XMP and IPTC: one with a single plain layer loses nothing, one with several layers, text, effects or masks is **flattened** (only the image as it looks remains), and the confirmation says so in bold. The formats EyeCanSee cannot write (HEIC, RAW…) can only use Save as, and so can images that would lose something: animated PNG, WebP, AVIF and GIF, TIFF files with several pages, and JPEG photos carrying more than one picture (the video of a motion photo, depth maps). **Ultra HDR** phone photos (Android 14 and later) are flipped and turned like the others instead: EyeCanSee turns their HDR map too, so they stay HDR. Those that are also motion photos (Pixel, Xiaomi) keep their video too, shown turned as the photo, without encoding it again. When an image cannot be saved over itself, Ctrl+S (or File > Save) says why in a window with a **Save as…** button. This is also the case in a folder opened through the file manager's connection (gvfs on GNOME, for example a camera, an iPhone or a network folder opened from the Files app): there replacing a file can lose the original, so EyeCanSee never saves over it. The same for a photo of a phone opened with a double click in File Explorer on Windows: Windows hands EyeCanSee a temporary copy of it, not the file on the phone, so saving over it would change only the copy; the status bar says so when it opens, and Save as… keeps the changes. On a phone or a server the changed image is first kept on this computer, in the app's data folder (`~/.local/share/eyecansee/edits` on Linux, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\eyecansee\edits` on Windows), and then sent: if sending fails or breaks off (cable pulled, connection lost), the original there stays as it was and a window says where the changed image is, with **Try again**, **Save as…** (somewhere else on the computer) and **Open the folder**. The kept copy is deleted once the image has been saved where it belongs. The same for Rotate and crop.
- The new file is written under a hidden name first, and only takes the image's name once it is complete; if something goes wrong, or the program closes half way, the original stays (or comes back, the next time the folder is opened) under its name. If the program closes just after the new file has taken the name, a hidden file `.<name>.eyecansee-old` may stay next to the saved image: it is the original. The next Save of that image sends it where a deletion would; until then you can delete it or rename it yourself.
- **File > Save as…** (Ctrl+Shift+S) opens *Save in another format* with the image already rotated: the original is not touched.

In the grid the same operations (the Image menu, or right-click > Transform) work on the selected image, or on all the marked ones, and save them straight away: EyeCanSee asks first, says where the originals go and warns that lossy formats may lose a little quality; images whose format cannot be saved back are left out, and so are animated and multi-page images and motion photos (not those that are also Ultra HDR, which are turned with their video), listed by name in the window that says, at the end, how many images were saved. Their thumbnails are then made again. When the only image chosen cannot be saved back (a PSD, a RAW…), the window says why and offers **Save as…**, which opens *Save in another format* with the image already turned.

### Editing an image

**File > New image…** (Ctrl+N) makes an empty image: a ready-made size (A3, A4, A5, Letter, Legal, Full HD, 4K, HD, square, upright for stories) or your own, in pixels, millimetres, centimetres or inches with the resolution (how many pixels it makes is shown under it), and a white, transparent or coloured background. It opens in the editor; the file does not exist yet, so its main button is **Save as…**, which asks for name, format and folder.

**Image > Edit image…** (Ctrl+E, also in the right-click menu) opens a page of its own for one image, to draw on it and to rotate and crop it. **Image > Rotate and crop…** opens the same page with the crop tool chosen. With several images selected they are greyed out (to turn several images together use the flips and turns above them or **Convert and resize**), and so are they for an image the viewer found animated or with several pages, of which only the first picture would be saved.

At the top the toolbar, a key for each tool: **Select** (V), **Arrow** (A), **Line** (L), **Freehand** (P), **Rectangle** (R), **Ellipse** (E), **Highlighter** (H), **Spotlight** (S), **Text** (T), **Speech bubble** (U), **Step numbers** (N), **Stamps** (K), **Magnifier** (Z), **Cover** (B); then **Crop and rotate** (C), **Turn left** and **right** (Ctrl+Shift+R, Ctrl+R), **Undo** and **Redo**; on the right **Copy**, **Close** and **Save copy**, with the arrow next to it for **Save as…** and **Overwrite the original…**. The tool in use is highlighted; pointing at a button shows its name and key, and on one that is off also why. Below, the properties bar changes with the tool: the crop controls, or the style of the object selected or of the one about to be drawn. At the bottom the status bar: file name and size of the result, a hint on the tool, the zoom with − and + (click on the percentage, 0 or a double click: everything in view) and the **Keys** button, which lists the keys and mouse moves of the tool in use.

- **Drawing**: with Arrow, Line, Freehand, Rectangle, Ellipse, Highlighter or Cover, drag on the image (Freehand follows the pointer and smooths the stroke; it is one object, which moves and stretches by its corners); with Shift held a rectangle is a square, an ellipse a circle. With Text, click where the text goes and type it in the field of the properties bar (Enter starts a new line); a text left empty goes away. A text (not on a curve) and a bubble are typed on the image itself, in a field over them where the mouse selects letters as in any field; while typing the letters are in the interface's font, and in their own once done. To change a text already written double-click it, or select it and change it in the field of the bar; Undo takes back the whole change. A selected line or arrow has three handles: its two ends and its middle, which bends it when dragged (dropped back in the middle, it is straight again). Arrows have four heads: filled, open, at both ends, dot. A selected text also has the choice of font among those installed: pointing at a name in the list shows the text in it at once, a click chooses it (also for the next texts), closing the list without choosing puts it back as it was; **Outline** draws a line round its letters, in a colour and width of its own, to read them on any photo; **Box** puts a box behind them, with its colour, opacity, margin and round corners (on a curve the box is a band along all the line); the **Rotation** slider tilts it; **On a curve** sets it on a line with two ends and a middle point, which bends it when dragged, and the letters follow it. With Speech bubble drag to draw the bubble and type its text at once, in the field of the bar (Enter starts a new line); it is rounded, oval, square, a cloud for thoughts (with round puffs for a tail) or spiky for a shout, with a border, a fill (also none) and its letters in a font, colour and size of their own; the text goes to a new line at the width of the bubble, which grows taller to hold it (dragging a corner the text fits the new width); its tail ends in a fifth handle, to drag where it points. With Stamps each click puts the icon chosen in the bar (check, cross, warning, information, question, star, thumbs up, and fifty more behind **…**: faces, hearts, arrows, flags, places, clocks, people and others), in the colour chosen; its size in the bar or by its corners. With Step numbers each click puts a disc with the next number (one more than the highest on the image); the number of a selected one can be changed in the bar, the disc takes the colour chosen and the number is black or white, whichever reads better on it.
- **Style**: eight ready-made colours and one of your choice (for a rectangle or an ellipse the colour of its **border** and of its **fill**, also none), the **eyedropper**: **I** takes a colour on the image, what is drawn on it included, with a box by the pointer as in the photo editors: a magnifier with the pixels around it (the one taken framed), the new colour over the one in use, the hex, RGB and HSL codes; beside the pipette, **1 px**, **3×3** or **5×5** take one pixel or the average of those around it; a click takes the colour, puts its hex code on the clipboard and shows the codes in the status bar for a few seconds; **Alt+click** with a drawing tool takes a colour and keeps the tool; its button and **Shift+I** take it from anywhere on the screen with the system's picker on Linux (on Windows with a click on the image, what is drawn on it included, or pressing on the image and letting go anywhere on the screen; a box by the pointer shows the colour under it, with its hex, RGB and HSL codes); width (the size of the letters for a text), opacity and line (solid, dashed or dotted), as each kind of object has them. The highlighter tints the photo as a real one does (it multiplies its colour into it): white becomes yellow, dark letters stay dark and readable; with less opacity the tint is lighter. As the filters it works on the photo, under the objects drawn. EyeCanSee remembers the last style of each kind of object, also after closing, with the width in proportion to the image.
- **Magnifier**: drag to draw it (with Shift a circle or a square); it shows the photo under it 2, 3 or 4 times larger, round or square, with a border of the colour and width chosen (width 0: none). It shows the photo with the filters and the cover boxes on it, wherever they are in the order, so it never shows what they hide; with **With the objects** it also shows, magnified, the objects drawn before it (those drawn after it stay over it at their size), sharp also on a turned lens.
- **Spotlight**: drag over what must stand out (with Shift a square or a circle; round or square in the bar): the rest of the photo is darkened, as much as the **Intensity** says. With several spotlights all their parts stay bright, and the rest is as dark as the darkest of them. As the filters, it works on the photo: the objects drawn stay bright over it.
- **Cover** is a solid box for private data (a number plate, an address, a face): nothing of what it covers is left in the saved file. It also covers what you drew before it; what you draw after it stays on top.
- **Select**: click an object to change it (the properties bar shows its style), drag it to move it, drag its corners or ends to resize it (with Shift held a box keeps its proportions); **Del** or the bin in the bar remove it. With a drawing tool a click selects only an object of its own kind (a step with Step numbers, an arrow with Arrow); anywhere else, also on other objects, the tool does its own work (Step numbers puts a number inside a rectangle or on its border). A rectangle or an ellipse with no fill is taken by its border, not by its empty inside.
- **Selection** (M): drag on the image to choose a part (Esc lets it go). **Ctrl+C** copies it, also to the clipboard for other programs; **Ctrl+X** cuts it, covering it with the colour of the empty corners (white without one); **Ctrl+V** pastes it as an object, a little aside of where it was, to move, resize and turn as the others, with its own opacity. Ctrl+V also pastes as an object an image copied in another program, in the middle of the result. The part copied is what is seen there: the photo with the objects drawn on it.
- **Filters** (the wand in the top bar): Blur, Pixelate, Sharpen, Black and white, Sepia, Brightness and Contrast, on the part chosen with Selection or on the whole image. A filter is an object as the others: it moves, resizes, changes its amount or goes until you save. It is already applied while you change it: **Done** in the bar (or Esc, or a click elsewhere) stops changing it and leaves it as it is; a click on the filter opens it again. It works on the photo, under the objects drawn; on the screen a preview of it is shown, in the saved file it is applied at full size. Blur and Pixelate also hide the HDR part of an Ultra HDR photo, as the cover box; but they are not enough to hide private text or numbers (at a low amount they can be read, or worked back), and the bar says so: for those use Cover.
- Every object stays one until you save: you can move it, change it or delete it at any time. **Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z) go back and forward through everything, the rotation and the crop included.
- **Copy** (Ctrl+C) puts the result, at full size, on the clipboard, to paste it into another program. On this page Ctrl+C does not copy the file and Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V do nothing (but in the text field).
- The wheel zooms around the pointer, + and − around the middle, dragging with the right button moves the view, a double click shows everything again.
- **Saving**: **Save copy** (Ctrl+S) always makes a copy next to the original, with the original's name and "(edited)" ("(edited 2)" when that name is taken), in the same format when EyeCanSee writes it back, otherwise a JPEG, or a PNG with transparency or from an OpenEXR or Radiance HDR file (a PSD too: the viewer's Save keeps it a PSD, the editor does not); the original is not touched; after Save copy the page stays open to go on, and the button becomes **Update copy**: it writes the same copy again, always from the original with all the changes (not from the copy, so nothing is compressed twice); if the copy is gone or another program changed it, a new copy is made and the status bar says so; and when you close it the grid selects the copy and the viewer shows it. Before writing, a window gives the copy's name and what it loses, if anything (it shows at once: the file is made after the answer, while the button waits, a large photo too): encoded again with a little loss, 8 bits per colour instead of more (a RAW, a 16-bit TIFF in JPEG), what is brighter than white (OpenEXR). The metadata stay, with the new sizes and without the small previews, and so does an Ultra HDR photo's HDR map, at least gain under every cover box, drawn object and magnifier, but not under the highlighter and the spotlight, which only darken or tint; the video of a motion photo is never kept (a motion photo that is also Ultra HDR, as Pixel and Xiaomi phones make them, keeps its HDR map without the video; with only flips and quarter turns it keeps the video too, as Save does). **Overwrite the original…** writes over it, after asking, and the page stays open; a second Overwrite starts again from the original as it was opened (kept in memory), replaces what was written before and leaves only the true original in the trash; the original always goes to a trash (the system's or the folder's own, also when deleting for good is chosen in the Options): if it cannot go there (for example a server that does not let EyeCanSee make the `.EyeCanSee-trash` folder) nothing is overwritten and the window of a failed save opens. It is off where Save never writes over a file (a folder opened through gvfs, a temporary copy of Windows) and for the formats EyeCanSee does not write back. **Save as…** (Ctrl+Shift+S) opens *Save in another format* with the result.
- **Phones and servers**: the status bar says that the image is edited in a copy on this computer. The copy (or the overwrite) is kept on the computer first, then sent: if sending fails, it stays there with **Try again**, **Save as…** and **Open the folder**, as for Save in the viewer. A photo of a phone opened from File Explorer on Windows is a temporary copy: Save goes straight to Save as.

#### Crop and rotate

- **Rotation**: the slider or the number next to it, from -180° to 180° by tenths of a degree; **Turn left** and **right** in the toolbar turn the image (and what is drawn on it, and the crop) by a quarter, **Back to 0°** sets the slider back. With Shift held, the mouse wheel on the image turns it by 0.1° a notch: handy for straightening a horizon against the thirds lines. The degrees can be typed with a dot or a comma.
- **Straighten**: click it, then draw a line along the horizon (or along something upright, such as a wall): when you let go the image turns to make that line level (or upright), and the button turns itself off.
- **Crop**: drag a corner or a side of the frame to resize it, its inside to move it. The proportions can be free, as the original, 1:1, 5:4, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9 or your own; the button with the two arrows swaps width and height. **Image only** makes the largest crop with no empty corners (it follows the rotation, and it is what you get at first); **Whole image** keeps all of the rotated image.
- **Empty corners**: where the crop goes past the image they are transparent (grey squares) or filled with the colour you choose. JPEG has no transparency: there they become white.
- **Apply** (or Enter) goes back to the tool before and shows the cropped result; Ctrl+Z undoes the crop. With this tool the whole image is shown, with the crop over it; with the others only the result. Dragging outside the frame moves the view. The size of the result is in the status bar.
- **Esc** does one thing at a time: it stops the eyedropper or a drag under way, ends the text being typed (a text still empty goes), lets go of the selection; when there is none of these left it closes the page. - **Close**, the window's ✕ or Esc close the page; with changes not saved they first ask whether to **Save** (a copy), **Save as…** or **Don't save** (**Cancel** goes back to the page).
- **Ultra HDR** photos of phones (with HDR brightness) stay HDR: their HDR part is rotated and cropped together with the photo.

## Copying and moving

**Ctrl+C** copies the selected items (or the image open in the viewer), **Ctrl+X** cuts them; then open the folder where they should go and press **Ctrl+V**. Each copy or cut is pasted once: to paste again, copy again. After copying something else in another program, Ctrl+V and Paste (in the menus and the toolbar) no longer paste the items copied in EyeCanSee. The same items are in the **File** menu and in the right click; the right click on a folder also has **Paste into this folder**. Files and folders, with everything inside them, go anywhere: another folder of the same disk, another disk, a phone, a server, also from one device to another.

- Copied items get a blue badge in the corner of their cell, cut items an orange one with scissors and fade; the status bar says how many are waiting for **Ctrl+V**.
- A window at the bottom right shows the file being copied, the destination folder, how many files and how much data have been copied, the percentage, the speed of the last seconds and the time left (worked out from the average), with **Cancel**: the file being copied when you cancel is not left halfway, the ones already copied stay. Towards a phone, Cancel waits for the end of the file being copied (a phone receiving half a file stops answering). Closing the window during a copy cancels it, and the window closes once the file being copied is finished.
- If an item with the same name is already there, EyeCanSee asks: **Replace** (for a folder **Merge**: its contents go into the existing folder, replacing the files with the same name), **Skip** or **Keep both** (the new one becomes "name (2)"). With several such items, a check box applies the same answer to all of them.
- Pasted into the folder they came from, copies become "name (2)"; a move does nothing.
- **Moving** on the same disk only changes the place, instantly. Between different devices EyeCanSee copies and deletes the original only after everything has been copied; the original does not go to the trash.
- A folder cannot be pasted inside itself, and nothing can be pasted into a trash or onto the list of a phone's storages.

## External devices

When you connect a phone, a camera, an external disk, a USB stick or an SD card, the **Devices** block appears at the top left, with its folder structure to expand like the tree. When you disconnect it, it disappears; if you were looking at it, EyeCanSee goes back to your home folder.

- **Android phone**: on the phone choose **Transfer photos** (PTP) or **Transfer files** (MTP). With Transfer photos you only see the photo folders (`DCIM`, `Pictures`); with Transfer files the whole storage.
- EyeCanSee connects to the phone directly, even if the desktop has already connected it (for example GNOME): this is much faster, especially in folders with hundreds of files. The device appears within a couple of seconds.
- On GNOME and KDE, opening the phone in EyeCanSee takes it away from the file manager On KDE EyeCanSee asks first: to take the phone it has to close KDE's kiod service, which also forgets the passwords of network folders open in Dolphin (it asks for them again next time). With Cancel the phone stays with KDE; F5 asks again. If you also open the phone in Dolphin while EyeCanSee has it, KDE gives up on it and does not see it again: when you close EyeCanSee or go to another folder, EyeCanSee puts KDE right by itself (if you said OK). Otherwise disconnect and reconnect the phone. To get it back, close EyeCanSee and click the phone in the file manager, or disconnect and reconnect it.
- A phone photo opened from the file manager (double click in the Files app on GNOME) opens through the direct connection too: EyeCanSee takes the phone from the file manager and says so in the status bar.
- **"EyeCanSee cannot open the device"**: another program holds the phone. If the desktop connected it, EyeCanSee switches to that connection by itself (slower: in folders with hundreds of files about one thumbnail per second); otherwise close the file manager and the other programs using it and press F5, or unplug the phone, plug it back in, choose File transfer and open it in EyeCanSee first.
- **Flatpak on KDE**: the phone can be held by a KDE service (kiod), also after Dolphin is closed and also when you plug it back in. EyeCanSee says so and offers **Release the phone**: close Dolphin and press it; EyeCanSee closes that service, and KDE starts it again when needed. Network folder passwords that were not saved are forgotten, and copies running in Dolphin stop, but they can be resumed with Retry. If KDE takes the phone back (Dolphin still open on it), EyeCanSee says so: close Dolphin and press Release the phone again. When EyeCanSee lets the phone go (closing it or going to another folder), it closes that service again, so that Dolphin sees the phone again.
- If the direct connection fails for a reason of its own and the desktop had already connected the phone, EyeCanSee opens it through that connection and says so in the status bar ("Opened through the file manager: slower"), with the error of the direct one. Without that connection the error is shown and EyeCanSee retries every 2 seconds.
- **"The device shows no storage"**: the phone was locked or set to "charging only" when it was connected: unlock it and choose File transfer. EyeCanSee retries by itself every 2 seconds and shows the storage as soon as it appears, no need for F5.
- **"The device was not found"**: the phone is reconnecting (it does so when you choose File transfer) or has been unplugged. EyeCanSee retries by itself every 2 seconds, no need for F5.
- When you open a directly connected phone, the block shows its folders and below there is an item to go back to your home folder.
- Photos taken, deleted or moved on the phone itself while its folder is open appear and disappear by themselves, a few seconds after the phone has finished (most phones say so to EyeCanSee). If the file was open in the viewer, the viewer closes and a message says which file was removed. A phone that does not say so: EyeCanSee finds out at the first read (opening the file, say), and the same happens.
- **Windows**: right after a photo is taken, or a file deleted, on the phone, Windows can stop talking to the phone for about six minutes: after 15 seconds a message says so, and closes by itself when the phone answers again. It is Windows' own driver. Unplugging the cable and plugging it in again (then File transfer) frees it at once. More in [Windows stops talking to the phone](https://github.com/IoSonoPiero/eyecansee/blob/main/docs/phone-stall.en.md).
- Thumbnails of devices stay in the cache after you disconnect them.
- External disks appear wherever the system mounts them. A partition the system did not mount by itself (for example because it has its own line in `/etc/fstab`) appears with a dimmed name: one click mounts and opens it. If needed, the system asks for your password.
- On Windows USB sticks, SD cards and disks connected via USB appear, with the name they have in File Explorer (for example "Backup (D:)"). Internal drives (C:, and any others) are under **This computer**: one click on another drive opens it. Phones connect through the Windows driver. Network drives and CDs/DVDs do not appear yet: open them with **File > Open location…** (for example `E:\`).

## Opening images

Type a folder, an image or a remote address in the address bar and press Enter, or start EyeCanSee with one. **File > Open location…** opens a window where you type the whole address, or choose the protocol and fill in server, port, user, password and folder (without a user, FTP logs in as `anonymous`):

```text
eyecansee                                  your home folder
eyecansee ~/Pictures/Holidays              a folder
eyecansee ~/Pictures/Holidays/beach.jpg    opens that image in the viewer
eyecansee ftp://user:password@nas.lan/Photos
eyecansee ftps://user@nas.lan/Photos
eyecansee sftp://user@server:2222/home/user/Photos
eyecansee sftp://my-nas/                   an alias of ~/.ssh/config
```

**Server manager** (**File > Server manager…**, like the site manager of FileZilla and WinSCP): the saved servers on the left, their details on the right: **Name** (empty = the server's name), protocol, server, port, user, password, folder and, for SFTP, **Key file** (**Browse…** opens the system window in the `~/.ssh` folder, or in your home folder without it) and **Key passphrase**, which is tried first. **New server** empties the fields, **Save** saves, **Delete** removes it after a confirmation, **Save and connect** saves and opens it. A saved server logs in **only** with the password and key saved here, never with the SSH agent or the system's keys in `~/.ssh`: if the key does not open (wrong passphrase, missing file) there is no login, and the server manager opens on that server to fix it. Every saved server appears in the left panel, in the **Servers** section below the devices: one click opens it, like a disk; right click: **Close connection** (if it is open you go back to your home folder; not during a copy) and **Edit…**. With a server or a phone open, the panel also shows **This computer** with your home folder and the last folder of the computer you were in. Going back to a server or to the computer from the panel takes you to the last folder you were in, while EyeCanSee stays open (with **Remember the scroll position of each folder** also to the same point). From there you copy and paste between servers, the computer, disks and phones as between two folders. An address that matches a saved server (same protocol, server, port and user), also typed in the address bar or reopened at startup, uses its password and key. **Master password**: the box at the top of the server manager says whether the list of servers is encrypted. Without a master password, passwords and passphrases are written **in plain text** in `servers.json` of the settings folder (on Linux readable only by you; the first time you save a server EyeCanSee says so in a warning). **Set a master password…** asks for the password twice and shows how strong it is (from red to green: at least "Strong" is needed); from then on the whole file is encrypted (`servers.vault`, Argon2id and ChaCha20-Poly1305) and every change is written at once, encrypted. EyeCanSee asks for it once per session, the first time you open a server or the server manager (in the panel, while locked, there is **Unlock the saved servers…**), and keeps it in memory until it closes. **Change…** replaces it, **Remove…** brings the file back to plain text after a warning. Every switch first writes the new file, reads it back and checks it, and only then replaces the old one: the file always stays whole. If you forget the master password the saved servers cannot be recovered.

Only one EyeCanSee runs at a time (a phone can be used by one program only): if it is already open, starting it again, for example with "Open with" on a photo, shows that folder or image in the window already open.

"Open with" offers EyeCanSee for images, the common video files (MP4, MKV, MOV, WebM, AVI), 3D models and CAD files (STEP, IGES, BREP, DWG, DXF and plot files included): on Windows the installer and the Microsoft Store package register them, on Linux the `.desktop` file does. It never makes itself the default program: that choice stays yours.

Remote folders behave exactly like local ones: the tree, the grid and the viewer work the same way. Passwords are never shown in the address bar.

With SFTP, on Linux and Windows, EyeCanSee reads `~/.ssh/config` as ssh does (aliases, HostName, User, Port, IdentityFile; not ProxyJump) and logs in with key files without a passphrase or with the password (never with the system's SSH agent; for a key with a passphrase save the server in **File > Server manager…**): if the server refuses, **File > Open location…** opens and asks for it. A password typed in the address is tried first. An address without a folder opens in the folder the server starts in (usually your home). A server's folder that no longer exists (moved, deleted, reopened at startup) gives way to the saved server's folder, or to the server's starting folder. The first time you connect to a server, EyeCanSee shows its key fingerprint apart, in a highlighted box, to compare letter by letter with the one the server shows, and asks whether to trust it; once trusted it goes into `~/.ssh/known_hosts`, as ssh does. A server whose key has changed is refused. With FTPS the server's certificate is checked as a browser does; one that no known authority vouches for (common with NAS boxes, which make their own) is shown with its SHA-256 fingerprint the first time, and once confirmed EyeCanSee remembers it. RSA keys work, but after a login with one EyeCanSee warns once per server: the RSA library it uses has a known flaw with no fix yet (RUSTSEC-2023-0071), so an ed25519 key is the better choice. EyeCanSee keeps one connection per server, and renames and moves (folders too) are done by the server itself.

| Ctrl+D | Find duplicates (see *Finding duplicates*) |
| Shift+arrows | Extend the selection |
| Shift+Del | Delete for good |
| Shift+F5 | Redo all the thumbnails of the folder |
| Tab | Next item; from the last one it moves to the devices |
| Shift+Tab | Previous item; from the first one it moves to the folder open on the left |

In the tree on the left:

| Key | Action |
|---|---|

| Right | Expands the folder; if already expanded, goes to its first subfolder |

| Shift+Tab | Previous folder; from the first one it moves to the last device |

In the devices: down and up change device (down on an expanded device enters its first folder), right and left expand and collapse it, Enter opens it on the right, Tab moves to the next one and from the last one to the computer's folders, Shift+Tab to the previous one and from the first one to the last item of the grid.

In the viewer:

|---|---|

The **Keys** button on the right of the status bar lists these keys. The mouse wheel zooms around the pointer, + and − around the middle of the window; Ctrl and the wheel always zoom, also when the wheel is set to change image (see *Options*). A zoomed image is moved by dragging it. It never gets smaller than fitted to the window, nor larger than the largest zoom set in the options (2000% at first); the status bar shows the zoom. The next image starts fitted again.

A thumbnail is made once and kept: it is made again only when the file changes size or date. If one looks wrong (for example made by an older version), right-click the image, or select several, and choose **Redo thumbnails** (also in the View menu). **Shift+F5** redoes all the thumbnails of the open folder.

The windows that tell how an operation went (the end of a conversion, of a copy, Check for updates), and About, close with **Enter** or **Esc**; in those that ask for a choice Enter confirms and Esc cancels.

Everywhere: **F1** opens this manual, **F5** reloads the folders, **F11** goes full screen and back; in the viewer full screen shows the image alone (or the video, model, drawing), without menus or bars, and Esc leaves it before going back to the grid. On a laptop with two graphics cards, a 3D model turned on a monitor of the other card can stutter in a window and runs smoothly in full screen. In the viewer F5 stays on the open image; if it has been deleted in the meantime, it goes back to the grid.

Folders on this computer update by themselves: images added, deleted, renamed or restored from the trash by another program appear and disappear without F5, also in the viewer, which stays on the open image. On phones too, when the phone says what changed (see External devices); on servers you need F5.

The same actions are in the menus: **View** (Open, Back to the grid, Next, Previous, First, Last, Parent folder, Refresh) and **Edit** (Cut, Copy, Paste, Select all). In the grid Next and Previous move the selection, in the viewer they change image. With several images selected, Enter or Open (also from the right click) open the first of them in the grid, and the viewer moves only among them, as the slideshow does, and the title counts only them.

A **right click** opens a menu with the actions for what is under the mouse, and selects it. The items follow the order of the menu bar. On files it shows the entries that work on the files chosen: Open, Slideshow (pictures and videos), Redo thumbnails; Transform (pictures), Save as… (one picture; in the viewer also a 3D model or a CAD drawing, as shown), Export as a 3D model (3D models), Convert and resize… (pictures and videos), Export drawing… (CAD drawings), Copy with the metadata…, Copy without sound…, Save the sound as a file… (videos); Cut, Copy; Rename (one file); then, apart at the bottom, Delete and Delete for good. In the viewer the same for the file open, with Back to the grid instead of Open and Save when you changed it. With files of different kinds chosen (for example a video and a 3D model), an entry that works on only some of them is marked with **\***, works only on those, and a line at the bottom of the menu says so; the menus of the menu bar do the same. On a folder (in the grid or the tree): Open, New folder inside…; Cut, Copy (and Paste into this folder, when something has been copied); Find duplicates…; Rename; Delete and Delete for good. On **..**: Parent folder. On an empty space of the grid: Paste (when something has been copied), Select all; New folder…; Parent folder, Refresh; Find duplicates… (inside the Trash also Restore all and Empty the trash).

In the viewer, after the last image EyeCanSee stops; with the option **In the viewer, after the last image start again from the first** it goes back to the first one, and the other way round. EyeCanSee loads the images around the current one in advance, so moving between them is immediate even on slow connections.

## Supported formats

- **Photos**: JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC/HEIF, AVIF, JPEG XL, TIFF, GIF (first frame), BMP.
- **Camera RAW**: ARW, CR2, CR3, CRW, DNG, NEF, NRW, ORF, PEF, RAF, RW2, SRW, 3FR, IIQ, ERF, KDC, MEF, MOS, MRW and others. EyeCanSee shows the full-size preview stored by the camera; if there is none, it develops the sensor data. The DNG files of Samsung Galaxy phones cannot be developed yet (they would come out white): EyeCanSee shows the full-size preview the phone stores in them.
- **Vector**: SVG, SVGZ.
- **Graphics and HDR**: PSD (merged image), ORA, OpenEXR, HDR, DDS, ICNS, QOI, TGA, ICO, CUR, PCX, JPEG 2000, PNM, XPM, XBM, SGI, WBMP, OTB, MPO, JPS.
- **Through ImageMagick**: PSB, XCF, PFM, Sun Raster, PICT, MIFF, MNG, JNG, DPX, Cineon, FITS and other less common formats (XWD on Linux only). ImageMagick also opens the PSD files the built-in reader does not handle (for example 32-bit ones).
- **3D models**: STL, 3MF, OBJ, PLY, glTF (GLB), FBX, Collada (DAE, ZAE), 3DS, OFF, AMF, VRML, X3D, IFC; STEP, IGES, BREP with the CAD engine. See *3D models*.
- **CAD drawings**: DWG, DXF, HP-GL (PLT). See *CAD drawings*.

Photos are rotated automatically using their EXIF orientation. The file extension is only a hint: an iPhone HEIC photo renamed to `.jpg` opens anyway.

## Language

EyeCanSee uses the system language when it is available, otherwise English. To force a language set `EYECANSEE_LANG`, for example `EYECANSEE_LANG=en eyecansee`.

## Known limits

- **CAD drawings**: seen from above; 3D solids are not drawn, viewports show the model from above and cut to their rectangle, hatches too dense to see show as a light fill, a line type too fine at the current zoom is drawn continuous, texts whose SHX font is not found use a system font, and a picture attached to the drawing whose file is not next to it (by its path or by its name) shows as a frame with a cross.
- **3D models**: one light and no surroundings are reflected, so metals look less shiny than in 3D programs; in OBJ, Collada, 3DS, 3MF, VRML and X3D only the base colour and its picture are used; glTF 1.0 files are not opened (only glTF 2.0). Animations are not played (models show in their rest pose); VRML 1.0 files are not opened, and texts in VRML and X3D use a system font.
- On **NTFS** disks (for example an external disk also used with Windows) the Files app (Nautilus) cannot restore from the trash, not even files it deleted itself; EyeCanSee reminds you in the confirmation. The files are in the trash all the same and can be restored from a terminal: `gio trash --list` shows what is there, `gio trash --restore "<trash:/// address from that line>"` puts it back.
- If the phone does not answer a request (seen on Windows: one deletion that took over five minutes, with everything else waiting behind it), the status bar says so with the seconds elapsed and the file: EyeCanSee carries on by itself as soon as the phone answers. The cause is not known: it happened twice on a POCO X7 Pro, deleting files just created, and does not repeat on demand.
- **Windows**: right after a photo is taken, or a file deleted, on the phone, Windows' own driver can stop talking to the phone for about six minutes; unplugging the cable and plugging it in again frees it at once. More in [Windows stops talking to the phone](https://github.com/IoSonoPiero/eyecansee/blob/main/docs/phone-stall.en.md).
- A phone opened through the file manager's connection (see External devices) is slower than the direct one: in folders with hundreds of files about one thumbnail per second.
- Images larger than 8192 pixels per side are shown downscaled.
- Files are sorted alphabetically: `img10` comes before `img2`.
- Hidden files and folders (starting with a dot, or on Windows with the "hidden" attribute) are
  not shown.
- HDR images (EXR, HDR, PFM) are shown without tone mapping and may look overexposed.
